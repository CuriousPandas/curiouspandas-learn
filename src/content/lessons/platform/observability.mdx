**Observability** is how you find out what a system is doing without stopping it. It rests on three signals:

- **Metrics:** numbers over time, such as requests per second or memory used. Cheap to keep, good for graphs and alerts. On the tilt platform: Prometheus, drawn by Grafana.
- **Logs:** what the program said, line by line. On the platform: Loki.
- **Traces:** the path one request took through several services, and how long each hop took. On the platform: Tempo.

Every other lesson breaks the thing you're watching. This one breaks **the watching**.

## Three empty dashboards on a healthy cluster

This happened three times on the tilt platform:

- Argo CD's monitor asked for a port named `metrics`, but every Argo CD service publishes `http-metrics`.
- Loki's monitor selected the label `app: loki`, but the chart had moved to `app.kubernetes.io/name`.
- cert-manager had no monitor at all.

Each time, Kubernetes was content, Prometheus reported nothing wrong, and eighteen panels read as a calm, healthy system.

## How a scrape is wired

With the Prometheus Operator, you don't edit Prometheus's config. You create a **ServiceMonitor**, which says "scrape the **Services** with *this label*, on the port with *this name*". Both are matched exactly. Get either wrong and the operator generates no scrape target, and it doesn't consider that an error. Nothing failed, so nothing alerts.

<Callout kind="tip">
The only place the truth appears is Prometheus's own **targets page**. When a panel is empty, open `/targets` before you open anything else.
</Callout>

## Existence is not freshness

Look at what the lab's grader checks. It asserts four separate facts on purpose, because the first can pass while the other three fail:

1. the ServiceMonitor exists;
2. the port it names actually exists on the Service;
3. Prometheus has a healthy target;
4. the data is **fresh**: the last scrape was recent.

"There's a series called `hello`" and "something scraped `hello` a moment ago" are different questions. Ask the second.

<Callout kind="real">
Prometheus also reloads its configuration on a timer, so a fixed ServiceMonitor can take up to a minute to show up. Testing straight away and concluding "my fix did nothing" is its own trap. Wait, then look at `/targets` again.
</Callout>
